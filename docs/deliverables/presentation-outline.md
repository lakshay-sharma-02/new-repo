# Presentation — Slide Deck

> 15–20 minute presentation. Content below each slide = what to say / show. Final screenshots and live demo included.

---

### Slide 1: Title
- **Laundry Roulette** — A Hostel Laundry Tracking & Accountability System
- Team members · Course · Date

### Slide 2: The Problem
> Hostellers face uncertain turnaround, zero visibility into their clothes after drop-off, and no recourse when things go wrong.

- 🎬 **Hook:** "Raise your hand if you've ever gone to the laundry room and your clothes weren't ready. Now raise your hand if that happened more than once. That's why we built this."

### Slide 3: What We Did
- Process: **Research → Synthesis → Ideation → Prototype → Test**
- Visual timeline: 10 interviews → 24 surveys → 3 observations → 18 ideas → working demo → 6 usability tests

### Slide 4: Research Methods
- 10 hosteller interviews (Weeks 3–5)
- 2 staff + 1 warden interview
- 3 full laundry cycle observations (B6, C3, A2)
- 24 survey responses across 5 blocks

### Slide 5: Key Finding #1 — The Black Hole
> "It's a black hole. You put clothes in, you get clothes out — maybe." — H01

- **10/10 hostellers** have zero visibility after drop-off
- 67% of survey respondents: "no visibility" is their biggest frustration
- Photo: observation of students checking the laundry shelf

### Slide 6: Key Finding #2 — The Numbers
- Satisfaction with laundry: **2.3/5**
- Turnaround predictability: **1.9/5**
- **75%** arrived to find clothes NOT ready
- **54%** missed a pickup because they didn't know it was ready
- **33%** gave up on a dispute entirely

### Slide 7: Key Finding #3 — Staff Want to Help
> "If there was a button I could press to say 'batch is ready' and it automatically tells students, that would save me so much time." — S02

- Staff use a notebook + tape tags; no way to communicate
- Wardens: "A tracking system would reduce complaints by at least half." (W01)

### Slide 8: Empathy Map
- Visual empathy map (from `design/empathy-map.md`)
- Highlight: **Feels** row — anxious, helpless, resigned

### Slide 9: Persona
- **Aarav, 2nd year B6** — the primary persona card
- "Just tell me when it's done. That's literally all I want."

### Slide 10: User Journey & Failure Points
- Journey map with the emotion curve dip
- 14 registered failure points → **top 5 highlighted**

### Slide 11: POV + HMW
- POV: A hosteller needs real-time laundry tracking because they have zero visibility and waste 2–3 trips per cycle
- **HMW:** How might we make hostel laundry predictable and accountable for both the student and the service?

### Slide 12: Ideation → Selection
- 18 ideas → weighted screening matrix (Impact ×5, Feasibility ×4, Desire ×3, Novelty ×2, Scope ×3)
- Winner: **Batch status tracking (81/100)**
- **Key insight:** No existing app targets hostel-batch laundry with per-hostel custom schedules

### Slide 13: Selected Concept
- **Laundry Roulette**
- One-liner: *Configure your hostel's schedule, submit laundry, track it through every stage, and get notified when it's ready — with built-in dispute reporting.*
- What we're NOT building: QR hardware, smart lockers, AI detection

### Slide 14–16: The Working Demo (live)
- **Screen 1:** Dashboard — stats cards, active batches, quick actions
- **Screen 2:** Submit + Track — status bar like food delivery
- **Screen 3:** Report issue flow
- **Demo tip:** Log in as `aarav@hostel.edu` / `demo123`, show a real status update with the "Mark as" button, show the toast + history timeline

### Slide 17: Usability Testing
- 6 hostellers, 4 tasks, all success criteria **met**
- Completion: timings 100% · submit 100% · track 100% · report 83%
- Ease-of-use **4.3/5** · Trust **4.5/5** · Would use **83%**

### Slide 18: Iteration (Before/After)
- Show 2 key before/afters: report-link visibility + dashboard stats cards (from `prototype/iteration-log.md`)

### Slide 19: Impact
- Students: no wasted trips, know when to come, evidence for disputes
- Staff: fewer "is it done?" interruptions, one-tap status updates
- Wardens: records to arbitrate fairly
- Any hostel works — custom timings

### Slide 20: Lessons Learned
- Observe the process — interviews alone miss reality
- Screen ideas against user desire, not novelty
- A simple system users will adopt beats a complex one they won't
- Future work: staff dashboard, push/WhatsApp notifications, photo evidence, real deployment

### Slide 21: Thank You / Q&A

## Presentation Tips
- Lead with the problem — make the audience feel it (start with a show of hands)
- Use real quotes — they add credibility
- **Demo live** — it's the strongest part of the project
- Keep data visual: bar charts for survey results, timeline screenshots for the journey
- End with the "so what": this turns an invisible, anxiety-inducing process into a visible, accountable one
